Lenses

A lens determines what angle of the world you capture and how blurry the background can get. Different lenses are designed for different purposes — portraits, sports, landscapes, close-ups.

What is Focal Length?

Focal length (measured in mm) tells you how "zoomed in" a lens is. A short focal length (e.g. 14mm) sees a wide angle — like peripheral vision. A long focal length (e.g. 200mm) sees a narrow angle — like binoculars.

Select a lens type above to see how it changes the field of view.

Types of Lenses

Prime Lens Fixed Focal Length

Prime Lens (e.g. 50mm, 85mm)

A prime lens has a fixed focal length — it cannot zoom in or out. You zoom with your feet by moving closer or farther.

Why use it? Primes are sharper, lighter, cheaper, and have wider maximum apertures (like f/1.4 or f/1.8). This means better low-light performance and more background blur.

Best for: Portraits, street photography, low-light, everyday carry.

🎭
50mm f/1.8
Classic portrait lens
Background blur ●●●●○
Field of view: 47°
Zoom Lens Variable Focal Length

Zoom Lens (e.g. 24-70mm, 18-55mm)

A zoom lens covers a range of focal lengths. You can go from wide to telephoto without changing the lens by rotating the zoom ring.

Trade-off: More convenient, but typically not as sharp as primes at the same price. Maximum aperture is usually narrower (f/3.5-5.6 for kit lenses).

Best for: Travel, events, general photography where you need flexibility.

✈️
24-70mm f/2.8
Professional travel zoom
Background blur ●●○○○
Field of view: 84° – 34°
Wide Angle Under 35mm

Wide Angle Lens (e.g. 14mm, 24mm)

Captures a very wide field of view — much wider than the human eye sees. Everything looks more dramatic and spacious.

Characteristic: Objects close to the lens appear large, objects far away appear small. Creates strong perspective distortion.

Best for: Landscape photography, architecture, interiors, astrophotography, environmental portraits.

🏔️
16mm f/2.8
Ultra-wide landscape lens
Background blur ●○○○○
Field of view: 107°
Telephoto 85mm and above

Telephoto Lens (e.g. 70-200mm, 400mm)

Reaches far subjects that you physically cannot get close to. Compresses perspective — background elements appear much closer to the subject than they really are.

Characteristic: Beautiful subject separation at longer focal lengths. Background blur is strong even at f/4 or f/5.6.

Best for: Sports, wildlife, birds in flight, candid street photography, concerts.

🦁
70-200mm f/2.8
Sports & wildlife zoom
Background blur ●●●●○
Field of view: 12° – 34°
Macro 1:1 Life-Size

Macro Lens (e.g. 60mm, 100mm)

Designed for extreme close-up photography. A true macro lens can focus close enough that the subject appears at 1:1 life-size on the sensor.

What that means: A 10mm insect fills the entire frame. A coin appears massive. Details invisible to the naked eye become clear photographs.

Best for: Insects, flowers, jewellery, food, stamps, small products.

🌸
100mm f/2.8 Macro
Extreme close-up lens
Magnification: 1:1
Minimum focus: 30cm
Fisheye Creative / Specialty

Fisheye Lens (e.g. 8mm, 15mm)

An ultra-wide lens that intentionally creates strong barrel distortion — straight lines curve dramatically, and the image has a distinctive circular/bubble look.

Used for: Creative artistic shots, skateboarding photography, action sports, 180° panoramas, surreal architectural shots.

Best for: Creative photography, action sports, music videos, artistic expression.

🌐

180° creative lens
Field of view: 180°
