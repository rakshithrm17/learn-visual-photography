Camera Types

From DSLRs to mirrorless to smartphones, cameras come in many forms. Understanding how they differ helps you choose the right tool — and understand why pros use what they use.

DSLR with Mirror
Mechanical shutter opening
Type 1

DSLR — Digital Single Lens Reflex

The traditional digital camera that dominated professional photography for decades. It uses a mirror and prism system — when you look through the viewfinder, you're literally seeing through the lens via a tilted mirror.

When you press the shutter, the mirror flips up, and the mechanical shutter opens to expose the sensor. This mirror mechanism is why DSLRs are bigger and heavier than mirrorless cameras.

✅ Strengths
  • • Optical viewfinder (natural view)
  • • Excellent battery life
  • • Huge lens selection
  • • Affordable used market
❌ Limitations
  • • Larger and heavier
  • • Mirror can cause vibration
  • • No silent shooting
  • • Slower AF in live view

Best brands: Canon EOS series, Nikon D-series

Mirrorless — direct path
Electronic Viewfinder (EVF)
shows live preview
Type 2

Mirrorless Camera

Mirrorless cameras removed the mirror entirely. Light goes directly from the lens to the sensor at all times. The viewfinder is an electronic screen (EVF) showing a live preview.

Without a mirror box, mirrorless cameras are much more compact. The lens mount is positioned closer to the sensor, which allows for new lens designs impossible with DSLRs.

Most new cameras released today are mirrorless. Canon RF, Nikon Z, and Sony E-mount are all mirrorless systems.

✅ Strengths
  • • Compact and lighter
  • • Silent shooting
  • • Superior autofocus (eye/face/body tracking)
  • • Better video capabilities
❌ Limitations
  • • EVF slightly delayed vs optical
  • • Battery drains faster
  • • Newer lens ecosystem (less variety)
  • • Generally more expensive

Best brands: Sony Alpha, Canon EOS R, Nikon Z, Fujifilm X, Olympus OM
