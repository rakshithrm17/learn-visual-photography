Module 1

What is a Camera?

A camera is a light-tight box with a lens at the front and a sensor (or film) at the back. The lens focuses light onto the sensor, and the sensor records it as an image. Simple as that.

The lens iris opening and closing

The Core Idea

A Camera is Like Your Eye

Your eye has a pupil that opens and closes to control how much light enters. A camera has an iris/aperture that does exactly the same thing.

Your retina records the image. A camera has a sensor that records the image electronically.

The difference is that with a camera, YOU control how much light enters and for how long.

Inside a Camera — The Main Parts

🔭

The Lens

Glass elements inside the lens bend and focus light onto the sensor. Different lenses change how much of the scene you capture.

⬡

The Aperture (Iris)

A set of overlapping blades inside the lens that open and close to control how much light passes through. You set the f-stop number.

⚡

The Shutter

A mechanical curtain in front of the sensor. It opens and closes very fast to control how long the sensor is exposed to light.

💡

The Sensor

A grid of millions of tiny light detectors (pixels). Each pixel records how much light hit it, creating the digital image file.

🔲

The Viewfinder

In DSLRs, a mirror reflects light up to the viewfinder so you see exactly what the lens sees before pressing the shutter.

🌟

ISO / Sensitivity

The sensor can be made more sensitive to light electronically. Higher ISO = brighter but noisier image. Lower ISO = cleaner but needs more light.

📚
The Three Controls That Matter

Every photo you take is controlled by three things: Aperture (iris size), Shutter Speed (exposure time), and ISO (sensor sensitivity). Together they're called the Exposure Triangle — and Module 2 teaches all three with live simulators.
