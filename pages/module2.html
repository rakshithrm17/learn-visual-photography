The Exposure Triangle

Three settings control every photo: aperture, shutter speed, and ISO. Change one and the others must balance out. Use the simulators below to see exactly how each one works.

The Triangle at a Glance

Every photo is a balance between these three. Change one, and the image gets brighter or darker. The skill is learning to balance all three for the result you want.

2A

Aperture — Depth of Field

Aperture is the size of the opening in the lens iris. A wide aperture (low f-number) lets in more light and blurs the background. A narrow aperture (high f-number) keeps everything sharp front to back.

Aperture (f-stop) f/2.8
f/1.4 (wide) f/5.6 f/22 (narrow)

Move the slider to see how aperture changes the photo.

💡
Wide Aperture (f/1.4 – f/2.8)

Background goes blurry. Subject "pops" out. Used for portraits, indoor photography, and low-light situations. Lets in the most light.

🏔️
Narrow Aperture (f/8 – f/22)

Everything is sharp from near to far. Used for landscape photos where you want the mountains AND the foreground flowers in focus.

2B

Shutter Speed — Freezing Motion

Shutter speed is how long the camera's sensor is exposed to light. A fast shutter freezes a moving subject sharply. A slow shutter creates motion blur — the subject streaks across the frame.

Shutter Speed 1/250s
1/4000s (fast) 1/60s 1s (slow)

Move the slider to see how shutter speed affects a moving subject.

⚡
Fast Shutter (1/500s – 1/4000s)

Freezes fast subjects. Birds in flight, athletes, water droplets. No motion blur — every detail is sharp. Needs more light to compensate.

💧
Slow Shutter (1/15s – 1s)

Creates motion blur. Silky waterfalls, light trails, streaking cars. Must use a tripod! Any camera movement also blurs.

2C

ISO — Sensitivity and Noise

ISO controls how sensitive the sensor is to light. Low ISO gives a clean, noise-free image but needs more light. High ISO brightens the image in dark situations but adds visible grain.

ISO Sensitivity ISO 100
ISO 100 (clean) ISO 800 ISO 6400 (noisy)
