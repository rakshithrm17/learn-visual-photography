Composition Rules

Composition is how you arrange elements within your frame. Even with a perfect exposure, a poorly composed photo won't look great. These rules help you create visually pleasing, intentional photographs.

📐 Rule of Thirds

Divide the frame into a 3×3 grid. Place your subject at one of the 4 intersection points rather than the center. The eye is naturally drawn to these points, making the photo more dynamic.

Tip: For landscapes, place the horizon on the top or bottom third — never the middle.

➡️ Leading Lines

Use natural lines in the scene (roads, fences, rivers, railway tracks) to guide the viewer's eye toward your subject or into the depth of the image.

Tip: Lines that start from a corner and lead toward the center are most effective.

🖼️ Framing

Use elements in the foreground (doorways, tree branches, tunnels, windows) to create a natural frame around your subject. This draws attention to the subject and adds depth.

Tip: The frame doesn't need to be complete — even partial framing works.

🔄 Symmetry

When a scene has perfect or near-perfect symmetry, place your subject in the center. Reflections in water, arched hallways, and long corridors work perfectly for this.

Tip: Break the symmetry intentionally with one element to create tension.

⬜ Negative Space

Leave large empty areas (sky, blank wall, open water) around your subject. This emptiness emphasizes the subject and creates a sense of minimalism and calm.

Tip: Have the subject facing into the negative space, not away from it.

📏 Fill the Frame

Sometimes the most impactful photo eliminates all background and gets as close to the subject as possible. Use a telephoto or macro lens to isolate textures and details.

Tip: Great for portraits, food photography, and texture shots.
